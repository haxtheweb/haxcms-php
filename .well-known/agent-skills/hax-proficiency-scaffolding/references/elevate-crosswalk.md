# ELEVATE-XR crosswalk (Dreyfus × Bloom's × learning loop × SCAFF)

Condensed from Stanney, Skinner & Hughes (2023): the stage descriptions come from the paper's text,
the objective, loop and dial rows from Tables 2 and 3. Wording is paraphrased; the learning loops
and dial settings are the paper's.

## Who is at each stage

| Stage | What they can do | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Novice | Absorbing facts and steps; no mental model yet, so cannot tell what matters. Prone to overload. | Focused, guided, step-by-step material; help noticing relevant features; rules to follow. |
| Advanced beginner | Recognizes the situation and recalls the rules that apply; starts forming rules of thumb. Still easily swamped because they cannot filter. | Gradually harder hands-on practice, choosing a course of action, linking new to known, specific targeted feedback. |
| Competent | No longer struggles with rules; makes plans and personal rules; starting to rely on judgment. | Authentic complex cases and inverse problems (work back from symptoms to causes), autonomy with support on request, "why" reflection. |
| Proficient | Sees the big picture and which cues matter; intuitive sense of the goal. | Complex, varied, unique cases; goal-setting; distractions; finding where cases contradict each other. |
| Expert | Works intuitively without rules; knows what matters and what will happen. | Uncertain, novel situations that challenge their understanding; self-critique; sharing knowledge with others. |

## Objective and activities

| Stage | Bloom's level | Example verbs |
|---|---|---|
| Novice | Remember facts, concepts, procedures | define, describe, identify, arrange, label, list, match, name, recall |
| Advanced beginner | Understand | compare, classify, differentiate, imitate, explain, translate, interpret |
| Competent | Apply | demonstrate, employ, examine, execute, illustrate, manipulate, use |
| Proficient | Analyze | deconstruct, differentiate, deduce, organize, relate, structure |
| Expert | Evaluate / Create | assess, contrast, critique, invent, hypothesize, validate |

## Learning loop (every loop ends with Repeat)

| Stage | Loop |
|---|---|
| Novice | Situate → Orient → Observe → Reflect by summarizing → Appraise via remembering |
| Advanced beginner | Situate → Guide → Organize → Reflect by exemplifying → Appraise via understanding |
| Competent | Situate → Give autonomy → Embody → Reflect by relating → Appraise via applying |
| Proficient | Situate → Goal-orient → Generalize → Reflect by contradicting → Appraise via analyzing |
| Expert | Situate → Challenge → Create → Reflect by self-critiquing → Appraise via evaluating |

What each new step means:
- **Situate**: place the content in a realistic context. Novice: a conceptual scenario; advanced beginner: a procedural one; competent: an integrative hands-on case; proficient: varied discovery cases; expert: novel, generative cases.
- **Orient**: direct attention only to the cues that matter for the mental model being built.
- **Observe**: mostly passive study of worked examples and demonstrations.
- **Guide**: model the correct behavior for the learner to imitate; help filter relevant from irrelevant cues.
- **Organize**: turn experience into situational rules of thumb ("when you see X, do Y"), including what things are for.
- **Give autonomy**: self-directed scenarios with subtle coaching on mistakes.
- **Embody**: perform the procedure for real or in simulation, where the actions are part of the task.
- **Goal-orient**: the learner sets the goal from the situation.
- **Generalize**: apply what they know to many differing cases; notice departures from the normal pattern.
- **Challenge**: test hypotheses, which cues matter, root causes, and alternative approaches.
- **Create**: produce a new solution or approach with no scaffolds.

## Reflection and appraisal by stage

| Stage | Reflection prompt type | Appraisal (formative assessment) |
|---|---|---|
| Novice | Summarize the gap between desired (correct) and actual performance | Recall: multiple choice, matching, completion, worked-example imitation; immediate right/wrong |
| Advanced beginner | Explain and give an example of correct performance | Understanding: "why" questions with explanations of the answer |
| Competent | Relate: why that decision, what followed, where else it applies; what is still unknown | Practical application, probing questions, spot the missing or wrong step |
| Proficient | Find consistencies and contradictions across cases | Analysis with self-explanation, plus peer assessment |
| Expert | Self-critique: other hypotheses, which cues mattered, root cause, better approaches | Self-assessment of complex, abstract work |

## SCAFF dial settings

| Dial | Novice | Advanced beginner | Competent | Proficient | Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stress | Low: no time limits, no consequences | Low: time awareness during instruction only, never during checks | Medium: some time pressure and consequences, nothing severe | High: significant time pressure and consequences, distractions | High: novel, uncertain, fast-paced, severe consequences |
| Complexity | Low: isolated topics, few steps, recognition before recall, worked examples | Medium: harder but not more abstract; assess situation, spot relevance, recognize consequences | Medium: harder and more abstract; no obvious solution | High: conflicting cues, compare approaches, deconstruct to generalize | High: synthesis, complex patterns, invent a solution to an ambiguous problem |
| Assistance | High: guided, no open exploration | Medium in Table 3, "high" in the paper's text: guide and imitate modeled behavior | Medium: support during self-directed work | Low: only as needed, after several errors | None: post hoc only; let them proceed past errors |
| Feedback | Immediate outcome-based (right/wrong, the correct answer) plus a brief why; the text calls it error-sensitive and elaborative; try again freely | Immediate elaborative (explanations), try again, progress shown | Immediate facilitative (advice, suggestions), self-explanation | Immediate strategy hints that do not give the answer | Delayed strategy feedback: point out inconsistencies |
| Fidelity | Low | Low visual (high physical feedback for motor skills) | Medium visual, high physical | High | High |

## Caveats to state when citing

- Proposed framework; the authors call for empirical evaluation.
- The headline ~30% gain comes from Claypoole et al. (2020), a non-peer-reviewed ASNE symposium paper co-authored by Stanney.
- Hours per stage (20, 40, 100, 2,000, 10,000) extrapolate from Kaufman (2014), Ericsson's work and an 80/20 heuristic. Treat as illustrative only; Macnamara et al. (2014) found deliberate practice explains far less variance in expertise than the 10,000-hour rule implies.
